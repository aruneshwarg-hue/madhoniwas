    Family history

    Saligram Parivaar · Shura Shah Parivaar of Manoharpur

    The family of Madho Niwas belongs to the Saligram Parivaar, a branch of the Shura Shah Parivaar of Manoharpur. Its story begins five centuries ago.

    1500Manoharpur

    Shura Shah Ji

    In or about 1500 AD, Shura Shah Ji was born at Manoharpur, about 42 km from Jaipur. He rose to become the Dewan, or Prime Minister, of Manoharpur.

    He had three sons, Udai Karan Ji, Aas Karan Ji and Madho Karan Ji. The three brothers married three sisters of Amarsar: Pokhari Devi, Rama Devi and Tulsi Devi.

    1562Samvat 1619 · Bhatner

    The battle of Bhatner

    In 1562 the three brothers fought bravely in the battle of Bhatner and were killed. Their bodies were carried back to Manoharpur on their horses.

    At Manoharpur their wives committed sati, and three chhatris were raised in their memory. The family's Sati Mandir at Manoharpur keeps that memory today.

    1868Manoharpur to Nasirabad

    From Manoharpur to Moonalal Ji

    Over the next three centuries the family moved from Manoharpur to Jaipur, with Seth Gumaniram Ji (b. 1748), and on to Nasirabad, with Saligram Ji (b. 1798), who gives the Saligram Parivaar its name.

    The line of Shura Shah Ji leads to Moonalal Ji, born in 1868, Head Clerk of the BB&CI Railways. He had four sons and three daughters.

    1894Nasirabad · Ajmer · Jaipur

    Rao Bahadur Madho Prasad Gupta

    Madho Prasad Gupta, Moonalal Ji's second son, was born at Nasirabad on 05.03.1894. He took his B.A. in 1914 and his LL.B. in 1926, and practised law at Ajmer until 1929, when he joined the Jaipur State Judicial Service as a Munsif.

    He was honoured with the title of Rao Bahadur, and in 1951 he retired as the first Registrar of the Rajasthan High Court. He and Saroj Devi had four sons and three daughters.

    1926Nasirabad · Jaipur

    Justice Dwarka Prasad Gupta

    Dwarka Prasad Gupta, the third son of Rao Bahadur, was born in 1926 and took his LL.B. in 1946. He was elevated as a Judge of the Rajasthan High Court in 1973 and as its Chief Justice in 1986.

    His second son, Aruneshwar Gupta, Senior Advocate in the Supreme Court of India, founded the Shri Shurashah Manoharpur Trust in 2020 to record the history and lineage of the Parivaar.

    TodayIndia & abroad

    A family across the world

    Five centuries after Shura Shah Ji, the Shura Shah Parivaar is spread across India and around the globe, from Jaipur, Delhi and Lucknow to the United States and Singapore.

    Moonalal Ji's children

    Four sons: Gordhanlal (1892), Madho Prasad (1894), Rambilas (1896) and Harbilas (1900). Three daughters: Ratni Devi (1906), Suraj Devi (1908) and Chanda Devi (1910).

    Shri Shurashah Manoharpur Trust

    Constituted at New Delhi on 20 February 2020 to collect and record the true history, ancestry and lineage of the Shurashah Parivaar across India and the world, to bring its members together, and to maintain the Sati Mandir at Manoharpur.
